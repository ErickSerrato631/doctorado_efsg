\section{Immunological Control}\label{sec:control}

In the spatial simulations, the active immunological reinforcement is prescribed by a Hill-type control law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u-hill}
u(\mathbf{x},t)=u_{\max}\,H_{\mathrm{act}}\!\bigl(c;K_c,n_c\bigr)\,H_{\mathrm{inh}}\!\bigl(i;K_i,n_i\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $H_{\mathrm{act}}(x)=x^{n_c}/(K_c^{n_c}+x^{n_c})$ and $H_{\mathrm{inh}}(x)=K_i^{n_i}/(K_i^{n_i}+x^{n_i})$, evaluated with the positive immune density when needed. Scenarios without Hill control set $u\equiv 0$, whereas scenarios with Hill control use the parameters reported in Table~\ref{tab:control-strong}. This control is not a global forcing; it is spatially targeted, becoming strongest where the cancer density is high and the immune density is still insufficient.

The control should therefore be read as a field-level coupling rather than as a shift of the homogeneous fixed point. At the reference state $(c^*,s^*,i^*)=(0,1,0)$ the activation factor vanishes, so $u=0$ locally and the listed eigenvalues are unchanged by turning the Hill field on. Its effect appears only after spatial fluctuations create regions with $c>0$, where the product of activation and inhibition gates changes the local reaction route and the gradients that enter the diffusive entropy production.

The qualitative flow geometry near the unstable homogeneous equilibria is summarized by the phase portrait in Fig.~\ref{fig:steady_states}, which is consistent with the projected coordinates $(c^*,s^*)$ in Table~\ref{tab:control-strong}. In this setting the role of control is not to create a stable homogeneous attractor. Instead, it changes where gradients and interfaces are produced, thereby modifying the spatial route by which the system approaches low-cancer regions. This is why the control effect is evaluated together with correlation lengths and diffusive entropy-production diagnostics rather than only through the local eigenvalue at the homogeneous state.
